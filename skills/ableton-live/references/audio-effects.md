## Operating rules and routing
- These are **Live UI procedures**, not a declaration of integration/API support. Inspect the actual device, installed Live version, edition, and available controls before acting. Different Live 12 builds and saved legacy devices can have different interfaces. Availability varies by license; Vocoder is unavailable in Intro and Lite. Do not infer other edition entitlements from this list.
- Start with a named outcome: tone shaping → EQ/filter; peak or envelope control → dynamics; repetitions → delay/Beat Repeat; captured performance → Looper; spaces → reverb; pitch correction of one audio voice → Auto Shift; fixed pitch/frequency translation → Shifter; played resonances → Corpus/Spectral Resonator; carrier/modulator synthesis → Vocoder. Spectrum and Tuner **measure; they do not process audio**.
- Before adjusting, identify the source track, device position, input monitoring, and whether this is an insert or return. For parallel returns, use fully wet output unless the device has a different control: Filter Delay uses minimum Dry, Beat Repeat uses Gate, and Looper normally uses Input → Output: Never. Do not confuse a sidechain's Dry/Wet or Mix with the audible output mix.
- Shared external-audio-sidechain procedure: unfold the device's sidechain panel; enable Sidechain/External; choose source and tapping point; set trigger Gain and internal/external mix; optionally filter the detector where offered. Audition the detector with its headphone/Listen control, then turn Listen **off** to assess processing. Detector gain changes sensitivity, not the source's mixer level. At 100% external mix the external signal alone triggers; at 0% the local signal does.
- Compressor, Gate, and Glue Compressor offer detector EQ; Multiband Dynamics offers external trigger mixing but no detector-EQ procedure here. Auto Filter and Roar have their own sidechain controls below. MIDI sidechains require note routing, not an audio detector; confirm source, tapping point, and actual arriving notes.
- Verification/safety procedure: retain the original settings; compare active/bypassed output at matched level over the same passage; inspect meters, response displays, and tail behavior. Check mono compatibility after widening. Start feedback, resonance, gain, and monitoring conservatively; reduce feedback/drive or disable the offending stage if levels rise unexpectedly. Do not assume an internal clipper protects later devices, dry paths, or final output. Do not claim listening or execution unless actually performed.

## Amp
- Use Amp for amplifier coloration; place Cabinet after it for an amp-and-speaker chain. Both also work independently on drums or synths. Choose **Clean** for the bright clean model, **Boost** for an edgier related model, **Blues** for bright breakup, **Rock** for classic rock drive, **Lead** for modern high gain, **Heavy** for vintage high gain, or **Bass** for strong lows and high-level fuzz.
- Set Gain for preamp drive, then Volume for the power stage. Blues, Heavy, and Bass can add substantial distortion through Volume as well. Bass/Middle/Treble and Presence interact with the modeled circuit: an EQ boost may change distortion or reduce another range. Recheck the whole tone after each change rather than treating the bands as independent corrections.
- Presence shapes mid/high power-stage edge; its effect varies by model. Choose mono Output unless stereo processing is needed; Dual costs twice the CPU. Set Dry/Wet for parallel coloration and verify level after model changes.

## Auto Filter
- Select the filter by the task: **Low-pass/High-pass** remove highs/lows; **Band-pass** isolates a range; **Notch** removes a narrow range; these offer 12/24 dB slopes. **Morph** moves low-pass → band-pass → high-pass, with 6/12/24/48 dB slopes. **DJ** uses a bipolar Control: negative values remove highs, positive values remove lows, with increasing resonance toward either extreme.
- **Comb** makes repeated spectral peaks/notches; Morph moves from peaks to notches. **Resampling** degrades sampling at the Freq rate; Res is inactive. **Notch + LP** combines a notch and low-pass at 12/24 dB; Morph moves the notch toward the cutoff, coinciding at 100%. **Vowel** uses Pitch in semitones instead of Freq, Formant to move through a/e/i/o/u instead of Res, and Morph to normalize formant gain progressively.
- Drag the response handle for Freq/Res, or Pitch/Formant for Vowel; use the output spectrum and left/right modulated curves to verify the motion. Set Drive before the filter. Circuit choices affect only Low-pass, High-pass, Band-pass, Notch, and Morph: **SVF** is clean at zero Drive; **DFM** feeds distortion back internally; **MS2** uses Sallen-Key behavior and soft resonance limiting; **PRD** uses a ladder circuit without explicit resonance limiting. Clip soft-clips the output; check its LED and compensate with Output, then set Dry/Wet.
- LFO: choose Rate in Hz, Time in seconds, synced divisions, or sixteenths; set Amt and Wave (Sine, Triangle, Saw, Square, Ramp Up/Down, Wander, S&H). Morph reshapes the waveform; S&H substitutes Smooth. Phase offsets same-rate L/R oscillators; 180° opposes their peaks, 0° matches them. Spin detunes their rates; 0% matches them, and Spin is inactive for Wander/S&H. Phase Offset shifts their starting point only in beat-based modes.
- For stepped motion choose quantization **Steps** and set the number of steps per cycle, or **S&H** and set the beat-time sampling interval. These quantization modes are separate from choosing an S&H waveform. For signal-driven motion use signed Envelope amount, Attack for rising levels and Release for falling levels; Hold completes the attack before release. Envelope S&H quantizes the follower to its S&H Rate.
- To follow another track, open the sidechain panel, enable External, choose source and Pre FX/Post FX/Post Mixer, then set Mix and SC Gain. SC Filter can isolate detector frequencies with the visible low-/high-shelf, peak, low-pass, band-pass, or high-pass choices; set Frequency, Q, and shelf/peak gain as applicable. Confirm the actual Type chooser rather than assuming extra filter types. Sidechain Listen temporarily replaces the processed output. Mono Sidechain is on by default; disable it in the context menu only when independent stereo envelope motion is wanted.

## Auto Pan-Tremolo
- Choose **Panning** for alternating L/R amplitudes or **Tremolo** for one amplitude modulation. Amount and modulation rate are shared between modes, not independent presets. Watch the position/amplitude display to verify which motion is active.
- Choose Sine, Triangle, Shark Tooth, Saw Up/Down, Square, Random, Wander, or S&H. Time modes are Rate (Hz), Time (100 ms–200 s), Synced, Dotted, Triplet, or 16th. Phase offsets channels (180° gives opposite phases); Spin progressively detunes them. Random/Wander/S&H allow Phase only. Beat-based modes expose Phase Offset; with random waveforms that control instead varies stereo width.
- Use Shape for sharper L/R transitions in Panning or rounded versus angular peaks in Tremolo; extreme shapes produce ramps. Invert flips the modulation, turning ramp gating into ducking. Tremolo's **Harmonic** alternates bands split at 600 Hz; **Vintage** adds nonlinear amplitude shaping. Lengthen Attack Time to let transients pass before modulation reaches full depth. Dynamic Frequency Modulation makes louder input accelerate the LFO at positive values and slow it at negative values.

## Auto Shift
- Feed a **monophonic** voice/instrument. Select High/Mid/Bass Pitch Range using the range LEDs and Input Pitch note/cents display; each range changes latency. Input Gain spans −24 to +24 dB. Read Latency, then enable Live Mode only if lower monitoring latency is needed; onset/fast-pitch glitches are a tradeoff, so disable it if those artifacts matter more.
- For correction to a scale, leave MIDI On off. In Quantizer, select Root/Scale or edit Included Notes; Use Current Scale follows the clip and disables manual Root/Scale. Set Correction Strength, then Smooth and Smoothing Time (0–200 ms). Check target note and correction cents. The Quantizer's Pitch Shift transposes **after correction in scale degrees**, unlike the separate semitone Pitch Shift.
- For played harmonies, show MIDI In, enable MIDI On, and choose an External Source MIDI track. Pre FX excludes source MIDI effects; Post FX/Post Mixer includes them. Mono uses one note with Glide between overlapping notes; Poly permits 2, 4, or 8 voices. **MIDI mode produces sound only while it receives notes**, subject to latch/envelope behavior; silence is first a note-routing check, not a gain problem.
- MIDI replaces Quantizer and disables scale awareness. To constrain notes, place Scale on the source and tap Post FX/Post Mixer. Watch the received-note keyboard. Set Attack Time 0–1,000 ms, Release Time 0–5 s, Note Latch to hold until the next Note On, and Pitch Bend Range 0–48 semitones. Route Velocity, Pressure, Mod Wheel, Pitch Bend, Note PB, or Slide independently to Pitch/Formant/Volume/Pan with each target's Mod Depth.
- LFO modulation works even without pitch correction. Reset retriggers after unpitched audio becomes pitched in Quantizer mode, or at a new MIDI note in MIDI mode; verify Onset/Trigger indicator. Set Delay 0–1.5 s, Attack 0–2 s, free-time or synced Rate, and Sine/Triangle/Triangle 8/Triangle 16/Saw Up/Saw Down/Rectangle/Random/Random S&H. Set independent depths for Pitch, Formant, Volume, and Pan.
- Global Pitch Shift uses semitones, Fine uses cents; Formant Shift spans −100% to +100% without transposing the fundamental. Formant Follow couples formant movement to pitch shifting. Vibrato Amount is 0–200 cents, Rate 2–15 Hz, with Fade In and Natural Vibrato for changing speed/depth. These also work without correction. Dry/Wet 100% gives processed audio; 50% can make a doubler, not transparent correction.

## Beat Repeat
- Set **Interval** for capture opportunities (1/32–4 Bars), **Offset** for their position, **Chance** for whether each happens, and **Gate** for the total repeat window in sixteenths. Example: Interval 1 Bar, Offset 8/16 starts halfway through the bar; Gate 4/16 repeats for one quarter note. Chance 100% always triggers; 0% never does. Repeat manually captures immediately and holds repetition, bypassing those scheduling controls until released.
- Grid sets each slice length; No Triplets restricts to binary divisions. Variation 0 fixes Grid; higher values randomize it, either on Trigger, every 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, or every repetition in Auto. Pitch lowers repeats by resampling and lengthens them rather than time-correcting them; strong shifts can disrupt the apparent rhythm. Pitch Decay lowers successive repeats; Decay fades their volume.
- Enable the combined high-/low-pass filter and set center/width if only a band should repeat. Choose **Mix** to add repeats, **Insert** to replace the original only during repeats, or **Gate** for repeats alone (useful on returns). Set Volume; verify both the repeated window and the return to normal input after Repeat/Gate ends.

## Cabinet
- Choose one of the five cabinet models with Speaker; labels such as 4×12 describe count and diameter in inches. Near On-Axis is focused/bright, Near Off-Axis less bright and more resonant, and Far includes a more balanced room impression. Dynamic adds grit; Condenser is more accurate. These are virtual microphones, not a hardware level limit.
- Put Cabinet after Amp for a conventional guitar chain; use either independently when speaker/amp coloration alone is the task. Output mono versus Dual trades stereo processing for twice the CPU. Set Dry/Wet. For multiple microphone perspectives, place Cabinet in an Audio Effect Rack, duplicate its chain, vary each mic position/type, and balance chain levels; verify that summing does not create unwanted level or phase changes.

## Channel EQ
- Use this for broad console-style tone control. HP 80 Hz removes rumble. Low is an adaptive 100 Hz shelf with ±15 dB gain. Mid is a ±12 dB peak with center frequency 120 Hz–7.5 kHz. High boosts as a shelf up to +15 dB; cutting to −15 dB also lowers a low-pass cutoff from 20 kHz toward 8 kHz, so cuts are not simply the reverse of boosts.
- Check the response/spectrum and compensate with Output. Place after reverb to shape its tone, on individual Drum Rack pads for local tone, or before Saturator for EQ-dependent channel-strip distortion. A large low boost before saturation increases distortion as well as bass.

## Chorus-Ensemble
- **Chorus** uses two modulated delays for light thickening; choose Auto Delay Time to scale delay with modulation, or fixed delay to keep it stable; Taps selects one or two. **Ensemble** uses three phase-distributed delays for denser motion. **Vibrato** varies pitch without the dry/delayed layering; its Dry/Wet and Feedback Invert controls are inactive.
- Enable the high-pass filter (20 Hz–2 kHz) to reduce modulation of lows. In Chorus/Ensemble, Width 0% is mono, 100% balances mid/side levels, and 200% makes the side chorus twice the mid level. Vibrato instead offers Offset (180° opposes channels) and Shape (0% sine, 100% triangle).
- Set Rate in Hz and Amount for time deviation, then Feedback for stronger resonance; inversion at high feedback makes a hollow response. Warmth adds filtering/distortion; match Output and set Dry/Wet, 100% on returns. Optional applications: Ensemble at 1–1.8 Hz and Amount 100% gives pronounced guitar motion; switching Feedback Invert above 90% feedback can create decaying oscillation bursts—treat this as a high-level-risk effect, not a default.

## Compressor
- Set Threshold so the intended peaks cross it, then Ratio: 3:1 passes a 3 dB increase above threshold as 1 dB; 1:1 does nothing. Read the orange gain-reduction meter and output meter. More than roughly 6 dB reduction can substantially reshape dynamics; loudness alone is not proof of improvement. Use Out or Makeup for level compensation and Dry/Wet for parallel compression.
- Knee 0 dB is hard; increasing it makes onset gradual. A 10 dB knee around −20 dB starts gently at −30 dB and reaches full compression at −10 dB. Transfer Curve shows this relationship; Activity shows input with either GR or Output history; Collapsed hides extra controls including Lin/Log.
- Attack controls time to full reduction; Release controls recovery, or enable Auto Release. A 10–50 ms attack preserves initial peaks; if they overload, shorten it, but extremely fast times may buzz or flatten attacks. Too-fast release may pump. Lookahead is **0, 1, or 10 ms**, trading latency for anticipation.
- Use **Peak** for short-peak response, **RMS** for less sensitivity to brief transients, or **Expand** to raise above-threshold dynamics (1:2 turns a 1 dB increase into 2 dB). Lin follows the set envelope times directly; Log releases heavily reduced peaks faster than lightly reduced material. Do not treat a compressor setting as an independently verified final-output ceiling.
- Apply the shared sidechain procedure for ducking: put Compressor on the background music/bass, not on the voice/kick trigger. For a mixed drum trigger, use detector EQ low-pass and audition Frequency/Q until the kick dominates. External sidechain disables automatic Makeup. Listen off → verify gain reduction follows the intended trigger and recovers between events. Processing algorithms changed in Live 9; old settings are not evidence of identical behavior across generations.

## Corpus
- Excite a physical resonator with incoming audio. Choose **Beam**, **Marimba** (beam with tuned bar overtones), **String**, **Membrane**, **Plate**, **Pipe** (one variable opening), or **Tube** (both ends open). Eco versus High Quality changes calculated overtone count/CPU; it does not apply to Pipe/Tube.
- Decay sets damping duration. Material moves from longer-lived lows toward longer-lived highs; Pipe/Tube substitute Radius, where larger values extend decay/high-frequency sustain and extreme values alter fundamental pitch. Bright and Inharm set overtone balance and compressed/stretched partial spacing; both are unavailable for Pipe/Tube. Opening applies only to Pipe (0% closed at one end, 100% open); Ratio controls Membrane/Plate aspect ratio.
- Hit places excitation from center toward edge; Pos. L/R place pickup positions similarly. These controls are absent for Pipe/Tube, measured at their open end. Width 0% mixes both resonators to mono; 100% separates them. Tune sets Hz, Spread detunes left upward/right downward for positive values (reversed for negative), and Fine supports MIDI tuning.
- LFO Amount modulates resonant frequency; choose Hz or tempo sync and sine/square/triangle/up/down/stepped-noise/smooth-noise. Phase 0°/360° matches channels and 180° opposes them; synced Offset shifts the start. Spin detunes rates only in Hz mode. Phase/Spin do not affect noise waveforms. Filter applies a post-resonator band-pass with Freq/Bdwidth.
- Bleed restores some unprocessed signal, useful at low tuning/quality, but is unavailable for Pipe/Tube. Set Gain and inspect the limiter LED. **Dry/Wet controls excitation, not immediate tail removal**: lowering it stops new processing but leaves sounding resonances.
- For MIDI tuning, unfold sidechain, choose MIDI From track/tap, enable Frequency, and choose Last or Low note priority. Tune becomes coarse transposition with Fine cents and PB Range in semitones. Off Decay makes Note Off damp the resonance: 0% ignores note-offs; 100% mutes immediately regardless of Decay. Verify notes change tuning and note-offs behave as intended; Frequency off restores Hz tuning.

## Delay
- Use independent L/R lines or Stereo Link; linked timing still permits separate Offset values. Sync buttons count **sixteenths** (4 = quarter note), with negative/positive Offset shortening/lengthening the delay. Time spans **1 ms–5 s**. Feedback normally recirculates each channel independently; Ping Pong alternates channels. Freeze holds the current buffer indefinitely and ignores new input until released.
- Enable the pre-delay band-pass; set Freq/Width or drag X for center/Y for bandwidth. Expand LFO Controls for Rate/Time/Synced/Triplet/Dotted/Sixteenth modes and Sine/Triangle/Ramp Up/Ramp Down/Square/S&H/Wander waves with Morph. Delay and Filter sliders set their independent modulation depths; check the flashing rate LED.
- Choose timing transitions deliberately: **Repitch** changes pitch like tape; **Fade** crossfades, useful for granular-like changes; **Jump** switches instantly and may click during tails. Hi-Quality in context/Show Options improves interpolation in Repitch/Fade and adds tape-like time smoothing in Repitch. Dry/Wet - Equal-Loudness changes the mixing law; use 100% wet on returns.
- Optional glitch setup: link channels, Sync 6, Feedback 50%, filter 2.86 kHz/Width 2.50, S&H 0.93 Hz, Delay modulation 66%, Filter 19%, Fade, Ping Pong off, Dry/Wet 50–80%. Optional chorus setup: unlink, left 12 ms/right 30 ms, Feedback 0%, filter 2.56 kHz/Width 8.50, Triangle 0.65 Hz, Delay modulation 58%, Filter 0%, Repitch, Ping Pong on, Dry/Wet 45–65%. These are starting examples, not mix requirements; verify timing, pitch artifacts, and level.

## Drum Buss
- Trim reduces input before all processing. Comp inserts a fixed fast-attack, medium-release, moderate-ratio compressor with makeup **before** distortion. Choose Soft waveshaping, Medium limiting, or Hard clipping with bass boost; Drive increases the level entering distortion.
- Crunch distorts mid/high content; Damp low-passes distortion-generated highs. Transients affects content above **100 Hz**: positive values add attack and sustain; negative values still add attack but shorten sustain. Do not describe negative Transients as simply removing attacks.
- Boom controls a resonant low-end enhancer; Freq tunes it and Force To Note selects the nearest MIDI-note frequency. Verify with Bass Meter/Boom Audition, then leave audition off. Decay changes low-frequency duration even at Boom 0% (post-drive/distortion input only); above 0% it also affects the resonant signal. Set Output Gain and Dry/Wet after shaping; recheck low-end peaks.

## Dynamic Tube
- Choose A for distortion that starts above a level threshold at low Bias, C for continuous rougher distortion, or B between them. Set Drive into the tube, Bias for nonlinearity, and Tone for the frequency distribution of generated distortion; extreme Bias can break up the sound heavily.
- Envelope modulates Bias: positive values dirty louder signals; negative values reduce their distortion for expansion-like behavior. Attack/Release shape that response but do nothing at Envelope 0. Match Output. Enable Hi-Quality in the title-bar context menu to reduce aliasing, accepting extra CPU; verify the actual benefit on the supplied signal.

## Echo
- Choose Stereo, Ping Pong, or Mid/Side and set the two corresponding delay lines in milliseconds or Sync Notes/Triplet/Dotted/16th. Stereo Link shares delay time, Sync, and Sync Mode but not individual Offset. Input sets dry gain; D enables input distortion. Feedback sets recirculation; Ø inverts the feedback.
- In the Echo tab, drag the tunnel to change delay time; rings represent repeats and the center dots are a fixed **1/8-note** reference. Enable HP/LP and set their separate cutoff/resonance, using the expandable filter display if helpful.
- Modulation offers sine, triangle, saw up/down, square, or noise in Hz or synced divisions; Phase 180° opposes L/R. Mod Delay sets time modulation; ×4 multiplies its depth for deeper short-delay flanging. Mod Filter affects cutoff. Env Mix 0% uses LFO only; 100% uses envelope only.
- Character: Gate rejects input below Threshold and closes with Release; Ducking reduces **wet output** while input exceeds its own Threshold and recovers with Release. Noise adds vintage-style noise with Amount/Morph; Wobble adds irregular time modulation with Amount/Morph. Repitch on bends pitch during time changes; off crossfades instead. Echo can stop processing at least eight seconds after input ceases; having both Noise and Gate enabled prevents that sleep behavior.
- Set Reverb amount, location (pre delay/post delay/feedback loop), and Decay; Stereo 0% is mono and >100% widens. Set Output and Dry/Wet, fully wet on returns; Equal-Loudness is in Dry/Wet's context menu. Verify gate/ducking thresholds with the actual input and distinguish a decaying tail from added Noise.

## EQ Eight
- Choose Stereo for a shared curve, L/R for independent channel curves, or M/S for mid/side editing; use Edit to select which visible curve changes. Up to eight filters per channel provide 12/48 dB-per-octave low cuts, low shelf, peak, notch, high shelf, and 12/48 dB-per-octave high cuts. Disable unused bands for CPU; stack equal bands only when a stronger response is intentional.
- Drag dots horizontally for Freq, vertically for Gain; for cut/notch filters vertical motion changes Q because they have no gain control. Alt/Option-drag adjusts Q; drag-enclose multiple dots to edit together, including with arrow keys. Select a numbered band to type Freq/Gain/Q. Expanded view exposes all bands together; Analyze toggles the output spectrum.
- Adaptive Q increases Q with boost/cut; Scale changes gain-capable bands only, not cuts/notches. Audition's headphone mode solos the held filter dot; release/disable it before assessing the whole signal. Match global Gain after EQ. Context-menu Oversampling runs at **2×** sample rate for smoother high-frequency behavior with extra CPU.

## EQ Three
- Use for DJ-style band isolation rather than transparent corrective EQ. FreqLo/FreqHi set crossovers; each band spans −∞ to +6 dB and has an independent on/off switch. LEDs indicate band activity above **−24 dB**, even when that band is off.
- Choose 24 or 48 dB crossover slopes; 48 dB is steeper, costs more CPU, and colors the signal even with all gains at 0 dB. If the bypass comparison reveals unwanted coloration, choose 24 dB or use EQ Eight rather than assuming unity knobs mean transparent pass-through.

## Erosion
- The current Erosion combines sine and filtered-noise modulation of a short delay. Set Freq and Amount using X/Y; Alt/Option-drag Y changes noise Filter Width. Noise Blend 0% is sine only, 100% noise only; Filter Width is inactive at 0%. Stereo Width 0–100% moves the modulation source from mono to stereo. Verify the input spectrum and modulation lines rather than assuming this is literal sample-rate reduction.
- Sets made before **Live 12.4** retain the older device as **Erosion Legacy**. Inspect the device name/version before using Noise Blend or Stereo Width; do not silently replace legacy processing.

## External Audio Effect
- Requires an actual hardware send/return loop and enabled interface channels. In Audio To choose the hardware output; Audio From chooses its return input; Configure opens Audio Settings if channels are missing. Establish the intended routing before raising send/return Gain. Reset and inspect both Peak indicators, and check the hardware itself for clipping. Use Invert only when return polarity needs changing; Dry/Wet is 100% on returns.
- Enable Options → Delay Compensation before adjusting Hardware Latency; otherwise the slider is disabled. Live cannot infer the hardware's additional delay. Set compensation from a measured round trip, not an invented number. Use **samples for digital connections** to retain sample count when sample rate changes, or **milliseconds for analog connections** to retain elapsed time. Samples allow finer adjustment; return to milliseconds before a sample-rate change if analog timing must remain constant. Verify alignment with a repeatable transient after any routing/rate change; exact interface calibration is setup-dependent.

## Filter Delay
- Use three filtered delay lines when different frequency regions need different echoes. Inputs are left, L+R, and right respectively; each line has its own enable, filter enable, output Pan, and level up to +6 dB. Filters also act in the feedback loops. Drag X for center frequency/Y for passband width.
- Sync numbers count sixteenths (4 = one quarter note); the percentage field offsets that time. Sync off uses milliseconds. Set Feedback conservatively because high values can self-oscillate; reduce it if repeats grow instead of decay. Minimum Dry makes a wet-only return; verify each line separately before combining.

## Gate
- Use Threshold to admit wanted sounds, then Return (hysteresis) to separate opening from closing levels and stop chatter near threshold. Flip reverses operation so below-threshold audio passes. Lookahead is **0, 1, or 10 ms**. Attack opens the gate; extremely short values may click, long values soften attacks. Hold delays closing after falling below threshold; Release sets closing duration.
- Floor is closed-state attenuation: −∞ mutes, 0 dB has no audible gating, intermediate values reduce rather than remove noise. Verify input/output traces and threshold/return lines. For a rhythmic pad gate, insert on the pad and use the drum track as sidechain; detector Gain/mix/EQ shape triggering, not the pad's output level. Listen shows sidechain level in green and replaces processed audio; switch it off before checking the actual gate result.

## Glue Compressor
- Choose for group/bus cohesion; Threshold/Ratio set reduction, but there is no adjustable Knee: it sharpens as Ratio rises. Attack is in **milliseconds**, Release in **seconds**. Release A combines slow baseline and fast transient recovery; it may respond too slowly to sudden level changes.
- Range caps reduction: 0 dB disables compression, approximately −60 to −70 dB resembles the original hardware's range, and −40 to −15 dB can limit effect strength instead of using parallel Dry/Wet. Match Makeup to the observed needle reduction, then verify by level comparison rather than trusting the estimate. Shared external sidechain Gain/mix/EQ/Listen controls can trigger bus compression from another signal.
- Soft clip is a colored waveshaper, not a transparent safety limiter. Nominal maximum is −0.5 dB, but **with Oversampling loud peaks may exceed 0 dB even when Soft clip is on**. Red Clip LED means >0 dB output; yellow means active soft clipping. Context-menu Oversampling processes at 2× sample rate to reduce aliasing/transient harshness at a CPU cost; check final peaks independently.

## Grain Delay
- Choose for granular echoes and pitch/time fragmentation. Set delay in milliseconds or Sync sixteenths (4 = quarter note), with percentage offset when synced. Frequency determines grain duration and changes how Pitch and Spray behave. Pitch transposes grains; Random Pitch moves from slight detuning to unstable pitch. Spray randomizes timing, from small smears to loss of rhythmic structure.
- Assign X from the row below the pad and Y from its left column; Delay Time can occupy X and Dry/Wet can occupy Y. Set Feedback cautiously because it can run away; if timing disappears unexpectedly, reduce Spray/Random Pitch and recheck Frequency before altering the source. Compare wet/dry at a safe level.

## Hybrid Reverb
- Signal path is input → selected reverb routing → EQ → output. Send changes reverb excitation without changing dry level. Predelay can be time-based or beat-synced; 1–25 ms is a useful natural-space range, not a required default. Each predelay time mode retains a separate Feedback value.
- Routing: **Serial** feeds convolution into algorithmic; Blend 100/0 gives convolution output, 0/100 gives algorithmic output excited by convolution. **Parallel** feeds independent engines and blends their outputs. **Algorithm** or **Convolution** isolates one engine, grays the unused controls, and makes Blend ineffective. Check routing before diagnosing a silent/inactive section.
- Convolution: choose IR category then file; arrows continue across categories. Categories include Early Reflections, Real Places, Chambers and Large Rooms, Made for Drums, Halls, Plates, Springs, Bigger Spaces, Textures, and User. Drag an audio file into the waveform for a custom IR; other audio in its folder joins the User list too. Removing/re-adding the device does not retain that temporary User list; repopulate by dragging the files again. Attack/Decay shape the IR envelope, Size changes its relative scale; verify the displayed waveform and preserve access to the actual files.
- All algorithms have Size, additional Delay, Decay (time to approximately −60 dB), Freeze, and Freeze In. Freeze prevents new input and sustains the tail; Freeze In admits more input into that frozen tail, potentially building level. Choose the algorithm by its behavior below, not merely by a preset name.

| Algorithm | Use and controls |
| --- | --- |
| Dark Hall | Smooth medium/long halls. Damping darkens; Mod adds motion and reduces fixed resonances; Shape moves from small/resonant toward large/diffuse. Bass X sets the low-decay crossover and Bass Mult scales that decay. Tiny Size plus long Decay yields metallic resonances. |
| Quartz | Clear early reflections and audible echo spacing for transient sources. Damping cuts highs, Lo Damp cuts lows, Mod adds motion, Diffusion sets tail density, and Distance moves from close/dense to distant/spaced reflections. |
| Shimmer | Pitch-shifted feedback with Damping/Mod, Pitch in semitones, Diffusion, and Shimmer intensity. Below 10% Diffusion approaches delay; Size spaces echoes on percussion. Shimmer 0% removes pitch shifting; 100% emphasizes the shifted feedback. |
| Tides | Moving multiband texture. Damping darkens; Wave morphs noise → sine → square at 0/50/100%; Tide sets motion depth, Phase offsets stereo modulation (180° opposing), and Rate uses synced divisions including triplet/16th/dotted variants. |
| Prism | Bright diffuse, noise-based space; small Size/Decay suits gated-like short tails. Low Mult/High Mult scale decay on either side of X over; longer settings suit sustained digital spaces. |

- EQ has four bands: low/high can be shelves or pass filters with 6–96 dB/octave slopes; the middle two are full-range peaks. Default placement is after both engines; Pre Algo moves it before the algorithmic engine in any routing. Output Stereo 0% is mono, >100% widens. Vintage has Subtle/Old/Older/Extreme degradation; Bass Mono sums output below **180 Hz**. Set Dry/Wet 100% on returns. Check frozen tails, bass level, and whether EQ is shaping excitation or the final tail.

## Limiter
- For a ceiling without deliberate coloration, set Ceiling and Input Gain, then watch Gain Reduction; below-ceiling peaks receive no reduction. **Maximize** changes Ceiling to Threshold and Input Gain to Output: lowering Threshold adds inverse gain after limiting, while Output sets the target level. Do not adjust a remembered label without checking which mode is active.
- Link 100% shares reduction between both channels to stabilize the image; 0% lets them react independently. Routing **L/R** processes left/right; **M/S** limits encoded mid/side then decodes, with more latency. Link still determines the degree of shared reduction in either routing.
- Release controls recovery; Auto analyzes the signal and disables manual Release. Lookahead is **1.5, 3, or 6 ms**: shorter settings reduce latency but may distort, especially bass; longer settings anticipate fast peaks better. Standard uses normal limiting, Soft Clip adds colored clipping near the ceiling (watch LED), and True Peak addresses inter-sample peaks.
- Verify final output, not only the device meter. Later processing or fader gain can defeat a limiter's ceiling; for final protection place it last on Main and keep Main volume below 0 dB. Excessive reduction is not repaired by raising Output; reduce input/Maximize intensity if transient damage is unwanted.

## Looper
- Before recording, choose Tempo Control: **None** decouples internal tempo, **Follow song tempo** follows Live, or **Set & Follow song tempo** lets the first recording establish Live tempo and follows later changes. Choose Song Control separately: None, Start Song (Play/Overdub starts Live; Stop does not stop Live), or Start & Stop Song. Starting Live through Looper also adjusts the phrase position of Link-connected apps; confirm this is intended before performance.
- With Live running, Looper follows its Quantization; with Live stopped, transport actions happen immediately. Record Length **x bars** records until another transport action. A fixed bar length with Live running ends automatically in Play or Overdub according to the adjacent selector; with Live stopped it determines the inferred tempo from the performed duration. Unspecified length can infer half/double the intended tempo—specify bars before the take when that ambiguity matters.
- Play replays the buffer; Overdub adds layers of its existing length; Stop stops playback. **Record/import replace existing audio, Clear erases it, and ÷2 discards the non-playing half**—do not use them as casual recovery. Clear during running Overdub retains tempo/length; elsewhere it resets them. Preserve a wanted buffer first through Drag me! to a track/browser; the resulting clip defaults to Re-Pitch warping. Dragging audio into that area replaces the buffer. ×2 duplicates the existing buffer to double its length.
- Multi-Purpose Transport Button: empty → Record; recording/overdubbing/stopped with material → Play; playing → Overdub. Double-click stops from Play/Overdub. Holding two seconds in Play invokes Undo/Redo; holding two seconds while stopped clears audio. Undo removes only overdubs since the latest entry into Overdub, preserving the original and earlier passes; Redo restores that removed layer. Map a footswitch in MIDI Map Mode and exit mapping before use; avoid unintended hold gestures.
- Speed changes playback rate **and pitch**; octave arrows halve/double speed. Reverse reverses existing material but newly overdubbed audio remains forward until Reverse is disabled, when the relationship swaps. Arrows and Reverse respect Quantization. Feedback affects old layers **only in Overdub**, beginning next repetition: 100% retains level, 50% halves it per pass; it has no effect in Play.
- Input → Output controls monitoring: Always passes input; Never is appropriate for wet-only returns; Rec/OVR passes it only during capture/overdub, useful with multiple pedal-controlled loopers; Rec/OVR/Stop excludes it only during playback. Verify that the monitoring choice is not duplicating the source.
- For an explicitly intended effect-feedback loop, first capture a pass, then create a second audio track whose top Audio From/To both name the Looper track and bottom choosers both select **Insert-Looper**; set Monitoring In, add effects there, then Overdub. Each pass traverses the extra effects. Confirm the exact Insert-Looper endpoints and start at restrained levels; ordinary unrestricted track feedback is not a substitute.

## Multiband Dynamics
- Use up to three frequency bands with independent **Above** and **Below** thresholds. Above + lower output = downward compression; Above + higher output = upward expansion. Below + lower output = downward expansion; Below + higher output = upward compression. Compression reduces dynamic range; expansion increases it. These are different operations, not synonyms for ratio strength.
- High/Low enable frequency splitting and set crossovers; with both off only Mid processes the full signal. Each band's activator bypasses its dynamics/gain, while Solo mutes the others. Input acts before dynamics, Output after. Small meters show input and large bars output; compare them to confirm whether the target range is being raised or lowered.
- Drag a block edge horizontally for threshold and inside vertically for ratio/effect strength. Ctrl/Cmd applies to all bands; Alt/Option pairs Above/Below within one band; Shift makes fine changes; double-click the block resets its amount. T/B/A switches expose Time, Below threshold/ratio, or Above threshold/ratio numerically.
- Above Attack begins when level rises over its threshold; Below Attack begins when it falls under its threshold. Release recovers after the opposite crossing. Soft Knee softens transitions; Peak catches brief excursions, RMS responds to sustained level. Global Time scales all attack/release durations proportionally; Amount 0% makes all ratios 1:1, not necessarily a gain bypass. Global Output trims the result. External sidechain follows the shared trigger-mix procedure.
- For normal multiband compression, choose crossovers and lower Above blocks (ratios >1). For de-essing, process only the upper band around **5 kHz**, with modest downward compression and relatively fast times; Solo temporarily helps tune the band. To recover contrast in squashed material, reduce Input for headroom, put Above thresholds below surviving peaks, then apply slight upward expansion; fast attacks emphasize peaks, slow ones soften that effect. This cannot recreate discarded information, and subsequent maximizing can remove the restored contrast again.

## Overdrive
- Set the pre-distortion band-pass with X for center/Y for width, then Drive and post-distortion Tone. **Drive 0% is not distortion-free**. Dynamics at low values adds more compression and makeup as drive rises; higher values preserve more dynamics. Use Dry/Wet for blending, 100% on returns; if the intended subtle result is already too distorted, reduce upstream level rather than assuming Drive can fully bypass the circuit.

## Pedal
- Start Gain low and increase with level monitoring; 0% still distorts, so use an upstream Utility to reduce input further if needed. Choose Overdrive for smoother warmth, Distortion for tighter aggression, or Fuzz for unstable breakup. Output compensates level; Dry/Wet adds parallel color. Hi-Quality in the title context menu reduces aliasing at extra CPU cost.
- Post-distortion adaptive EQ: Bass is a 100 Hz peak; Mid selects **500 Hz, 1 kHz, or 2 kHz**, with broader range at higher settings; Treble is a **3.3 kHz** shelf. Sub boosts below **250 Hz**. Sub on/Bass −100% and Sub off/Bass +100% are different shaping options. For more precise EQ leave these neutral and follow with EQ Eight. Compression before Pedal makes drive more even; boosted/resonant pre-EQ makes it more selective and intense.
- Optional task-specific setups: long-decay kick → Distortion + Sub, then use high Mid position for attack, Bass for weight, Treble cut for less air; drum fizzle → Fuzz, Gain 50%, Sub off, Bass/Mid −100%, Treble 100%, Output −20 dB, blend up from Dry/Wet 0%; broken-speaker texture → Fuzz, Sub off, Bass minimum, Treble 25%, Mid 100% at 2 kHz, Gain 100%; sub harmonics → Overdrive + Sub/Bass, gradually raise Gain. These are deliberately colored examples, not universal artistic defaults; check output and stop short of unwanted breakup.

## Phaser-Flanger
- **Phaser** uses moving all-pass notches: Notches sets their count, Center their center frequency, Spread their spacing/Q, and Blend sends modulation from Center at 0 to Spread at 1. **Flanger** uses modulated short delays/feedback; Time is the reference and movement right in its display means shorter delay. **Doubler** simulates layered takes with bipolar delay modulation; movement right means longer delay. Do not interpret both displays identically.
- Unfold for two LFOs, envelope follower, and Safe Bass. Main LFO is Hz or synced, with Sine, Triangle, Saw Up/Down, Rectangle, Random, Random S&H, Triangle Analog, Triangle 8, or Triangle 16. Triangle Analog changes with rate; 8/16 are stepped triangles. Phase offsets L/R (180° opposed); Spin detunes their rates. Duty Cycle compresses motion toward the cycle start at +100% or end at −100%; 0% is neutral and random waveforms are unaffected.
- LFO2 is always triangular with independent free/synced rate; LFO2 Mix 0% uses main only, 100% uses LFO2 only. Amount scales modulation from both. Enable Env Fol and set nonzero Envelope Amount for signal-level modulation, negative to invert; Attack/Release determine response. Safe Bass reduces processing below its **5 Hz–3 kHz** cutoff.
- Feedback and Ø inversion intensify the response; high settings can cause rapid level jumps and Doubler can leave audible echoes. Reduce feedback immediately if growth is unintended. Match Output, use Warmth for distortion/filtering, and Dry/Wet 100% for a return.

## Redux
- Treat downsampling and bit reduction separately. Lower **Rate** for more imaging/inharmonic content; Jitter randomizes the sample clock and adds noise/stereo width. Pre filters before resampling and also narrows Jitter width. Post low-passes afterward; Post-Filter Octave measures octaves relative to **half the Rate frequency**.
- Lower **Bits** for fewer amplitude levels and less dynamic range. Shape gives small amplitudes finer resolution as it rises, so its result depends on input dynamics. DC Shift offsets amplitude before quantization and may increase level/crunch, especially at low Bits. Set Dry/Wet, fully wet on returns; if the source becomes an almost square wave, restore Bits or reduce the blend rather than expecting lost dynamics to survive.

## Resonators
- Feed five parallel resonators through a low-pass/band-pass/high-pass/notch input filter with Frequency. Mode A favors realistic resonance; Mode B changes the character particularly at low root pitches. Decay normally lasts longer for lower notes; Const makes decay independent of pitch. Color sets brightness.
- Resonator I receives L+R; II/IV receive left and III/V right. With no scale/tuning, Note sets root **C−1–C5**; with scale awareness it is relative to the active clip's scale in octave 2; with a tuning system it selects an absolute tuned note index. II–V follow I and transpose independently by **±24 semitones** or **±21 scale degrees**, with individual fine cents tuning. I's Fine adjusts the root precisely.
- Each resonator has Gain and on/off; turning one off saves CPU, and disabling I does not stop II–V. Width affects only the wet II–V signal, collapsing it to mono at 0%. Gain acts on the resonated signal, not on a fully dry output. Set Dry/Wet and verify the root/interval units before assuming semitone values in a scale-aware or tuned Set.

## Reverb
- First shape excitation with low/high cuts: X moves band center, Y changes bandwidth; disable unused filters to save CPU. Early Reflections' Spin adds Amount/Rate modulation; excessive rate creates Doppler pitch/panning. Shape low gives gradual early decay with earlier diffuse onset; high separates a faster early decay from later diffusion and can improve clarity.
- Diffusion Network creates the tail; high/low shelves change frequency-dependent decay, not simply final output EQ. Diffusion/Scale set density/coarseness and strongly color tiny rooms. Chorus adds separate modulation to the tail with Amount/Rate. Disable unused shelves, Spin, or Chorus when appropriate for CPU savings.
- Predelay delays first reflections; **1–25 ms** is a natural-space example range. Size sets room volume; tiny rooms can sound metallic, very large ones diffuse/delay-like. Smooth None changes delay lengths immediately and may artifact; Slow/Fast smooth Size changes. Decay measures time to **−60 dB**. Density trades Sparse CPU savings against High richness; Stereo spans mono to **120°** independent reverberant channels.
- Freeze sustains diffusion; Flat bypasses decay shelves while frozen, otherwise those bands keep losing energy. Cut stops new excitation entering the frozen tail; Cut off adds input. Reflect/Diffuse independently balance early and late levels; Dry/Wet balances the whole effect. If a tail keeps growing, check Freeze/Cut and input before simply reducing Decay.

## Roar
- Set Drive for overall input to all stages. Tone Amount positive emphasizes highs/reduces lows; negative does the reverse around Tone Frequency. Color Compensation applies a mirrored post-shaper curve so you can alter what saturates without imposing the same final EQ; negative Tone with compensation can preserve low-end impact while changing drum saturation.
- Choose routing by required interaction: **Single** one stage; **Serial** Stage 1 then 2 (Blend moves from Stage 1 to their combination); **Parallel** independent stages (Blend balances them); **Multi Band** three stages with Low/High crossovers; **Mid Side** independent center/side processing; **Feedback** independently shapes input and recirculation; **Delay** sends delayed Stage 1 into Stage 2 without first scaling by feedback, then recirculates Stage 2 with feedback gain. Single/Multi Band have no two-stage Blend. Each stage has its own enable.
- Within a stage set Shaper Amount and watch the input reach nonlinear parts of the curve. Shaper Bias makes asymmetry; extremes can silence the signal, so restore Bias before diagnosing routing failure. Shaper Level compensates curve-dependent gain. Shaper enable bypasses just that shaper. Choose curves deliberately:

| Shaper family | Behavior and useful distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Soft Sine / Diode Clipper / Tube Preamp | Smooth saturation; Diode also attenuates highs, Tube Preamp retains more transient dynamics at high Amount. |
| Digital Clip / Bit Crusher | Hard clipping adds high-order harmonics; Bit Crusher lowers quantization bits and increases companding with Amount, responds strongly to Bias and quiet input. |
| Half Wave Rectifier / Full Wave Rectifier | Half creates asymmetry; Full adds an octave-up character. |
| Polynomial / Fractal / Tri Fold | Polynomial morphs toward complex metallic shapes; Fractal adds strong upper harmonics; Tri Fold is a harsher triangular wavefolder. |
| Noise Injection / Shards | Noise Injection adds stereo noise then smooth distortion; Shards changes curve-segment slopes for rhythmic breakup. |

- Select LP/BP/HP/Notch/Peak/Morph/Comb/Resampling/Dispersion filter and cutoff. Resonance exists except for Resampling; Peak has gain and Morph interpolates filter shapes. Comb creates moving notches, Resampling adds sample-rate artifacts, and Dispersion delays different frequencies differently without changing their levels. **Pre** puts filtering before saturation, so it does not remove new harmonics generated afterward; otherwise filter those harmonics after shaping.
- Open Mod Sources/Matrix. LFO 1/2 offer sine/triangle/square/up/down, Free Hz or Synced/Triplet/Dotted/Sixteenth, Morph, and Smooth. Env follows input with Attack/Release, Hold to complete attack, Threshold/Gain, and Frequency/Width detector filtering; Input Listen auditions that detector. Noise provides Simplex and Wander for differently interpolated random motion, S&H for stepped values, and Brown for low-pass-filtered noise; set free/synced Rate and Smooth.
- With Matrix open, click a target control, then drag source/target cells for depth. Global Modulation Amount scales assignments. Expand the header view to see stages/sources together and inspect colored activity LEDs. The matrix X erases all assignments; do not use it as a casual diagnostic reset.
- Feedback modes Time/Synced/Triplet/Dotted make delays; Note tunes ringing. Set Amount, Invert, and feedback band-pass Frequency/Width. The loop compressor reduces feedback during loud input. Feedback Gate makes recirculation fade after input ends; with it off feedback can persist indefinitely. Compression Amount affects output and consequently feedback; Compressor Sidechain HP Filter reduces low-frequency influence on that compressor.
- Output Gain affects wet level **before hard clipping and Dry/Wet mixing**. Match level after Drive changes and do not treat that clip stage as final-output protection. For external envelope triggering, enable External SC, select Post FX/Post Mixer, and set Mix/SC Gain. For played feedback pitch enable MIDI > FB Note, route MIDI Pre FX/Post FX; Feedback Mode and Feedback Amount become inactive. Verify the active panel before adjusting a disabled control; turn Sidechain Listen off to hear Roar's output.

## Saturator
- Pick Analog Clip for rounded clipping, Digital Clip for hard clipping, Soft Sine/Medium Curve/Hard Curve for differing saturation, Sinoid Fold for folding, Bass Shaper for heavily driven low frequencies, or Waveshaper for custom shaping. Analog/Digital Clip remain linear below their clipping points. Use Drive and the curve/input display to see when nonlinearity begins.
- Bass Shaper Threshold spans **0 to −50 dB**: lower values give softer clipping and can suit high-drive bass; higher values are harder. Set Post Clip Mode Soft Clip or Hard Clip to constrain the processed output to Output; Soft Clip applies Analog Clip at the output. This is not a substitute for checking the final mixed signal and later chain gain.
- Color applies a pre-shaper EQ and its inverse afterward. Amt Lo negative reduces low-frequency saturation and positive increases it. In expanded view, Amt Hi affects a second region selected by Frequency/Width; use the curve handles or numeric controls. This changes which frequencies distort, not just their final EQ. Match Output and set Dry/Wet, 100% for returns.
- Waveshaper's expanded **Drive** is separate from input gain: 0% removes the extra curve parameters' effect, 100% fully applies them. Curve emphasizes third-order harmonics; Depth sets the superimposed sine amplitude; Period its ripple density; Linear reshapes the central linear segment with Curve/Depth; Damp flattens near-center samples like an extremely fast gate. Check that this additional Drive is nonzero if those controls seem ineffective.
- Context options: Hi-Quality reduces aliasing at extra CPU cost; Pre-DC Filter removes DC before nonlinear processing. Verify output level and unwanted high-frequency artifacts rather than assuming a more complex curve sounds better.

## Shifter
- Choose **Pitch** to transpose by semitones/cents without intentionally remapping harmonic spacing, **Freq** to add a fixed Hz offset (small values phase/tremolo, large values become metallic), or **Ring** to add/subtract the selected frequency. Drive exists only in Ring. Coarse/Fine units follow mode; do not treat Hz as semitones.
- Wide reverses the right channel's Spread offset, making opposite shifts; it has no effect at Spread 0. In Pitch mode, longer Window generally suits lows and shorter Window highs. Enable Delay for echoes, choose its free or beat-synced mode using the displayed units, set Feedback, and use Tone to cut highs from feedback. Verify timing in the installed UI before translating values into automation.
- LFO supports Sine, Triangle, Triangle Analog, Triangle 8/16, Saw Up/Down, Rectangle, Random, and Random S&H. Set Amount, Hz/synced Rate, and Duty Cycle; synced Offset shifts the cycle start. Periodic waves offer Phase/Spin, Random offers Phase, and Random S&H offers Width (0% identical channels, 100% fully stereo opposing behavior). Phase 180° opposes channels; Spin detunes rates.
- Enable Env Fol for input-level modulation; Attack/Release follow rising/falling level and Amount uses semitones in Pitch or Hz in Freq/Ring. Unfold sidechain for Internal versus MIDI pitch control; select the MIDI track, Glide in ms, and PB **0–24 semitones**. Confirm notes arrive before attributing a fixed pitch to modulation failure; use Dry/Wet for the final blend.
- Applications: duplicate a drum layer and use Pitch+Delay for shifted echoes while preserving the main track; for phasing use Freq shifts around **2 Hz or less** and approximately 50% mix; for tremolo use Ring below roughly **20 Hz**, optionally Wide with small Spread for stereo motion. Match the added layer's level.

## Spectral Resonator
- For a fixed resonant pitch use Internal; for played resonances use MIDI and select source/tapping point. An external controller must first enter a MIDI track selected as that source. Mono uses one voice and Glide; Poly uses **2–16 voices**. MIDI Gate on requires notes; off permits audio-driven resonance without notes, but **Poly always gates**. PB range is **0–24 semitones** and MPE is supported.
- Internal Freq can use Hz or notes; Use Current Scale changes it to SD Shift relative to the active clip, with chromatic fallback when no scale is active. MIDI Transp. spans **±48 semitones** or **±28 scale degrees** when scale-aware. Tuning systems work in both modes and substitute note indices. Verify the displayed unit before setting a transposition.
- Stretch compresses/expands spacing between resonant partials; at **100% only odd harmonics** remain. Shift transposes the **input spectrum**, not the resonator, by ±48 semitones. Decay sets sustain; HF Damp/LF Damp frequency regions follow resonator pitch or incoming notes.
- Modulation: None is static; Chorus uses triangle motion (at Mod Rate 0 it changes only amplitudes); Wander uses random sawtooths; Granular generates irregular decaying partial envelopes with Mod Rate controlling density. Pch. Mod is bipolar despite its positive-only readout, except Granular's positive-only envelopes. Choose rate/depth to fit the requested motion.
- Spectrogram shows dry yellow/wet blue. Harmonics increases brightness and CPU; in Poly its total is divided across voices, so more voices means fewer partials per voice. Quantize aligns partials to the active scale/tuning, or chromatic pitches without either. Input Send has a limiter LED; Unison adds detuned copies and Uni. Amt sets their spread. Wander/Granular modulate unison voices separately. Use 100% Dry/Wet on returns.
- Applications: tune drums or vocoded-like vocals through MIDI; derive a pitch guide with Convert Melody/Harmony to New MIDI Track, then route it in and verify the conversion; low Freq with Unison/Wander and modest Mod Rate/Pch. Mod makes reverb-like tails; two instances in series with different MIDI sources build layered harmonics. If silent, check Poly/MIDI Gate and note routing first; if thin in a large chord, inspect harmonics-per-voice before raising gain.

## Spectral Time
- Enable Freezer and/or Delay; choose **Frz > Dly** or **Dly > Frz** explicitly rather than assuming the order. Spectrogram dry is yellow and wet blue. Lower Resolution reduces latency at the cost of accuracy/fidelity; context-menu Zero Dry Signal Latency removes dry-path delay instead of aligning it to processed output, useful for live monitoring but a different dry/wet timing relationship.
- Freezer requires **Freeze engaged in both Manual and Retrigger modes**. Manual captures on the button with Fade In/Out in ms. Retrigger → Onsets captures detected transients (Sensitivity 0% least, 100% most sensitive); Retrigger → Sync uses Interval in ms or beat time. Crossfade transitions old/dry into the next freeze with X-Fade relative to the interval; verify its displayed unit. Envelope uses separate Fade In/Out in ms and allows up to **eight overlapping freezes**.
- Delay Time modes are milliseconds, Notes, or counts of 16th/16th Triplet/16th Dotted. Feedback recirculates; Shift adds another frequency shift on each repeat. Tilt positive delays highs more, negative delays lows more. Spray randomizes delay across frequencies; Mask positive confines Tilt/Spray to highs, negative to lows; Stereo controls their width. The Delay section's Dry/Wet affects **only that section**.
- Input Send sets input level; global Dry/Wet blends the entire device and should be 100% on returns. Verify active section/order, Freeze state, and which Dry/Wet control was changed before diagnosing a missing effect. Reduce Resolution for tracking only if its fidelity tradeoff is acceptable.

## Spectrum
- Place at the point to be measured; it does not alter the signal. Choose Channel left/right/both. Higher Block sample count increases frequency accuracy and CPU; faster Refresh increases temporal responsiveness and CPU. Avg 1 exposes individual blocks/short peaks; higher Avg smooths readings over time.
- Graph switches interpolated curve versus frequency bins. Max retains accumulated peaks, reset by clicking the display while enabled or by restarting the song. Scale X linear helps inspect highs; logarithmic and semitone use the same spacing but Hz versus note labels. Hover for amplitude/frequency/note readings.
- Range mode permits manual amplitude bounds, vertical scrolling, and horizontal zooming on the left scale; Auto follows incoming level and disables those controls. Expand from the title bar or double-click the display. Keep scale, averaging, and analysis position consistent when comparing signals; a changed display is not necessarily a changed sound.

## Tuner
- Feed a clean **monophonic** source; chords, noise, and dense harmonics can mislead pitch estimation. Tuner only reports pitch—it does not correct it. Set Reference deliberately, **410–480 Hz**, default **440 Hz**, before adjusting the instrument.
- Classic Target: centered ball is in tune, right is sharp, left flat; arrows indicate adjustment direction. Classic Strobe: rightward rotation is sharp, leftward flat, faster movement means larger error. Histogram shows pitch over time: above the note's gray center line is sharp, below flat. Green/red signal in/out of tune.
- Toggle Hz/Cents for absolute frequency versus offset. Histogram drag vertically scrolls pitch, horizontally zooms; Auto centers incoming pitch. Right-click the display for Sharps, Flats, or Sharps and Flats spellings. If readings wander, simplify/clean the input and verify Reference rather than blindly retuning to every detected overtone.

## Utility
- Use Gain for level automation without occupying the track fader: range **−∞ to +35 dB**; arrow keys step 1 dB between −18 and +35 dB and accelerate toward −∞ below −18. Balance positions the output. Mute placed before delay/reverb stops excitation while allowing downstream tails, unlike end-of-chain track muting.
- Left/Right Phase switches invert channel polarity. Channel Mode Left or Right discards the other input and sends the chosen one to both outputs; Width and Mid/Side then become inactive. In stereo processing, Width 0% sums to mono and >100% widens; Mono explicitly sums the stereo input.
- Width's context menu selects Mid/Side Mode: 100M is mono mid, 0 is the center setting, and toward 100S emphasizes side; 100S outputs only side with opposite-polarity L/R. Verify mono compatibility after changes. Bass Mono sums lows below an adjustable **50–500 Hz** cutoff; use Bass Mono Audition to check that band, then disable it.
- DC removes DC offsets and extremely low frequencies. Its audible benefit depends on such content being present, especially before nonlinear processing; it is not a universal tone enhancer. Verify gain, channel choice, and Mute state when the downstream chain becomes unexpectedly quiet.

## Vinyl Distortion
- Use Tracing Model for even harmonics and Pinch Effect for odd harmonics. Each X/Y area sets frequency horizontally and drive vertically; Alt/Option-vertical changes Q. Global Drive scales both models. Choose Soft for dub-plate-like behavior or Hard for conventional record distortion.
- Pinch Stereo creates opposite-phase distortion components for a wider vinyl-like result; Mono avoids that stereo component. Crackle Density sets event density and Volume sets noise level. Verify that intentionally generated crackle, rather than source noise, explains sound during otherwise quiet passages; check mono after stereo Pinch.

## Vocoder
- Requires an edition with Vocoder (not Intro/Lite). Put it on the **modulator** audio track, usually speech/drums. For a singing synth, put a harmonically rich synth on another track, set Carrier **External**, select that synth via Audio From/Post FX, and provide simultaneous notes and modulator audio. Arm both tracks for live input as needed; solo the modulator track to audition only vocoding. No useful carrier or no modulator activity can mean no useful result.
- Other carriers: **Noise** uses internal noise (X lowers sample rate toward the left, Y lowers density downward); **Modulator** resynthesizes the source itself; **Pitch Tracking** uses a monophonic oscillator with High/Low tracking limits, saw or three pulse shapes, and Pitch offset. Pitch Tracking holds its last clear pitch until another is detected; polyphonic/noisy input or oscillator resets can produce unexpected pitch changes.
- Enhance normalizes carrier spectrum/dynamics for brightness. Unvoiced adds noise for consonants; Sens. 0% uses main carrier only, 100% keeps unvoiced noise always on, and Fast/Slow sets detection switching speed. Raise Bands for finer analysis at more CPU cost; click band meters to attenuate individual bands. Range bounds the bank; BW 100% is the most accurate overlap, lower/higher values are more selective/overlapping. Precise keeps equal band gain/width; Retro narrows and boosts upper bands.
- Gate silences bands below threshold. Depth 0% discards the modulator envelope, 100% is conventional vocoding, and 200% emphasizes only high-level peaks. Attack/Release follow modulator amplitude; very fast values preserve transients but may distort. Mono treats both signals as mono; Stereo uses mono modulator/stereo carrier; L/R processes both in stereo. Formant shifts carrier filter frequencies, Level sets output, and Dry/Wet blends the result.
- For a formant-shifter setup without a separate synth, choose Carrier Modulator, Depth 100%, Enhance on, then adjust Formant and the filter bank. If articulation is poor, verify a harmonically rich carrier, sensible Range/BW, Unvoiced/Enhance, and non-excessive gating before increasing output level.
