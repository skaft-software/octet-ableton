## Scope and preflight
- These are Live UI workflows, not promises that an integration can perform them. Inspect available tools before automating; use verified UI access for unsupported operations, or report the limitation.
- Check the installed Live version/edition, device availability, plug-in format, and actual control names before changes. Do not infer an edition entitlement or a particular Live 12 point-release feature from a missing browser entry.
- Plug-in formats covered here are VST2, VST3, AU2, and AU3; AU is macOS-only and AU3 requires Live 11.2 or later. Other operating-system, codec, architecture, and edition requirements need local verification.
- Safety/verification practice: preserve a recoverable Set state, identify the exact track/clip/device/parameter, and prefer narrow changes. Obtain approval before broad clears, overwrites, configuration removal, or whole-arrangement moves. No listening, rendering, or Live execution is implied by this reference.

## Device chains and comparison
1. Open a selected track's Device View by double-clicking its title bar. The lower-right Clip/Device View toggles allow both views to be stacked. Show/hide shortcuts are Ctrl+Alt+3/4 on Windows and Cmd+Option+3/4 on macOS, respectively.
2. Load a browser device/preset by double-clicking, or select a destination track then press Enter on the browser item. With no track selected, loading can create a track. Dragging also works into tracks, drop areas, or Device View; dropping a sample into an empty MIDI Device View normally creates Simpler, subject to sample-dropping defaults.
3. Order processing left to right. Audio effects accept audio on audio, return, and Main tracks; on MIDI tracks use **MIDI effects → instrument → audio effects**. Instruments convert MIDI to audio; do not put MIDI effects downstream of that conversion.
4. For external live input with **Auto** monitoring, arm the track to hear input through its devices. Inserting a MIDI instrument normally arms its track automatically; verify instead of assuming input is present.
5. Drag title bars to reorder or move devices between tracks. Copy/duplicate target the selected device; paste inserts **before** the selected device. Select the blank area after the final device, or move selection there with the right arrow, to paste at the end. For an approved removal, selecting a device title and pressing Delete/Backspace targets that device; bypass is the non-removing comparison method.
6. Inspect inter-device input/output meters to locate a vanished or weakened signal. Bypass the suspect device with its Activator to compare: bypass leaves the signal unprocessed and avoids that device's processing CPU cost. Device-chain headroom is effectively unrestricted, but physical outputs and written audio files can clip.
- Double-click a device title bar or choose **Fold** to save space. Expanded-view arrows may open a panel above the device (Roar's Gain Stage/Modulation Matrix; EQ Eight's Frequency Display) or within it (Phaser-Flanger's LFO/Envelope Follower).
- Open a title-bar context menu with right-click or **Show Options**. Some controls are device-specific: scale awareness only where supported, Chord's Learn, Auto Filter's Mono Sidechain. Do not assume every device has the same menu.
- For A/B comparison on supported built-in devices, keep a reference in one state and edit the other. Both start at the loaded defaults; edits initially affect A. Use **Compare: Copy A to B**, then **Compare: Switch to B** or P; the title gains `(B)`. Equivalent commands work in the other direction.
- A/B comparison is not available for Racks, Max for Live devices, or plug-ins. It compares parameter states, not whole Rack configurations.
- Automation is state-specific. Switching A/B disables the affected automation; switching back does **not** restore it. Select the intended state and use the parameter's **Re-Enable Automation**. Another state switch disables it again.
- Copying/duplicating a device or saving its preset retains only the selected state's values and initializes **both** destination states from those values. Do not use those operations to archive two alternatives.
- Leave **Options → Delay Compensation** on for ordinary work; it aligns processing delays, including returns. **Reduced Latency When Monitoring** is available while compensation is on: enable for lower input-monitored latency at the possible expense of alignment with still-compensated tracks/returns; disable for alignment at potentially higher monitoring latency.
- A tempo-synced device after a latency-producing device can still sound misaligned with Live's clock. Large Track Delay values or plug-in-reported latency can cause sluggishness; compensation also adds CPU load. Disabling compensation is a diagnostic tradeoff, not a normal cure, and makes Track Delay controls unavailable.

## Presets and defaults
- A browser device folder loads its default preset; its children are other presets. Navigate folders with left/right and items with up/down, then Enter to load. Browser loading is preferred; Live-specific `.adg`, `.adv`, and `.amxd` files can also be dragged in from the system file manager.
- To replace a preset, drop the replacement directly over the current device, or select the device and enter **Hot-Swap** with Q/the title-bar button. Without a selection, Q targets the first audio effect on an audio track or the instrument on a MIDI track. Swap only within the same signal/device type, not an audio effect for a MIDI effect.
- In Hot-Swap, choose and Enter/double-click a preset; choose the parent device folder for its default. Exit with Q, Esc, either Hot-Swap X button, or by leaving the browser for another view. If `-EnableHotSwapOnSelection` is already enabled in `Options.txt`, selecting an item replaces the sound immediately rather than waiting for Enter.
- Sample Hot-Swap preserves a sample-targeting workflow: hover the lower-right Drum Rack pad, Drum Sampler Waveform Display, Impulse slot, or Simpler Sample Display; Sampler's button stays visible at the expanded Zone Editor's upper left. Enter/double-click a sample in the opened browser location. Exit with its button, browser X, Q, Esc, or another view.
- **Save Preset** stores current settings and custom Info View text in the matching User Library `Presets` folder. Name it and Enter to confirm; Esc cancels. Alternatively drag the title bar to a folder under Places. If **Collect Files on Export = Ask**, sample-referencing presets prompt about copying files; resolve dependencies intentionally.
- Defaults are persistent workflow settings, not one-off presets. User-wide defaults live under User Library `Defaults`; project-specific defaults take priority for Sets in that Project. Before replacing a default, identify its scope and preserve the previous configuration.

| Need | Procedure and important distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Device/Rack startup values | Adjust it, then title-bar **Save as Default Preset**. Applies to Live instruments, MIDI/audio effects, and all Rack types. It goes under `Defaults/Audio Effects`, `Instruments`, or `MIDI Effects`. A sample in an instrument default also loads. |
| Drag-saved device default | Drop into the corresponding default device folder and keep the original device name, such as `Simpler`; a custom name will not be recognized. Replacing an existing default prompts for overwrite. |
| Plug-in panel configuration | Add controls in **Configure**, then **Save as Default Configuration**. `Defaults/Plug-In Configurations/Audio Units` or `VSTs` is created as needed; AU and VST versions can have separate defaults. This saves the exposed parameter list, **not parameter values**. Save a containing Rack preset/default to retain values too. |
| New audio/MIDI track | Configure devices and mixer settings, including routing, volume, pan, and sends; use **Save as Default Audio Track/MIDI Track** on its title. Drag-saving into `Defaults/Creating Tracks/Audio Track` or `MIDI Track` requires the exact name `Default Audio Track` or `Default MIDI Track`. |
| Dropped sample instrument | Configure an empty Sampler or Simpler, then drag it into `Defaults/Dropping Samples/On Device View` or `On Drum Rack`. A pad's **Save as Default Pad** also targets `On Drum Rack`. If several presets are present, the most recently added is used. |
| Slicing audio into MIDI | Build a Drum Rack containing one chain with an empty Sampler/Simpler on a pad; add effects and Macro assignments as needed. Save it under `Defaults/Slicing`; select it in **Slicing Preset** in **Slice to New MIDI Track**. |
| Harmony/Melody conversion | Save an instrument or, for MIDI/audio effects and Macros, an Instrument Rack under `Defaults/Audio to MIDI/Harmony to MIDI` or `Melody to MIDI`. |
| Drums conversion | Save a Drum Rack with Sampler/Simpler hits under `Defaults/Audio to MIDI/Drums to MIDI`. A Drum Rack nested in an Instrument Rack is not recognized for this purpose. For each conversion folder, the most recently added default is used. |
| Project-only workflow | Create `Defaults` and the relevant matching subfolders under Current Project; drag devices/tracks there. The normal **Save as Default…** commands still target the User Library, not this Project. |

- Project-only plug-in configuration needs a special transfer: save the configuration normally, then transfer its plug-in folder containing `Default.appc` into the Project's matching `Defaults/Plug-In Configurations/Audio Units` or `VSTs` location. Directly dragging the plug-in into Current Project does not work. A browser move removes the User Library copy; preserve it first or save it again if both scopes need it.
- Factory behavior returns when the corresponding custom default is removed from the User Library. Treat removal as a scoped configuration change requiring approval, not as a troubleshooting first step.

## Plug-ins: discovery and control
1. Open **Settings → Plug-Ins → Plug-In Sources** (or **Activate** under the browser's Plug-Ins label). No sources enabled can explain an empty list. Instruments have keyboard icons and belong on MIDI tracks; audio effects belong on audio paths.
2. Windows: choose the **VST Plug-In Custom Folder**, confirm the displayed path, and enable **Use VST Plug-In Custom Folder**; a path may already have been found in the registry. macOS: enable system-folder scanning and/or a custom folder. VST system locations include `/Library/Audio/Plug-Ins/VST` and the corresponding user Library location. Do not mistake that VST location for a verified VST3 path.
3. Subfolders are scanned. Aliases to other local plug-in folders can be placed within the configured VST source folder, including aliases to other drives. On macOS, enable **Use Audio Units** for AU discovery.
4. After an install/uninstall performed outside this workflow, use ordinary **Rescan** or restart Live. For launch-crash diagnosis, hold Alt (Windows)/Option (macOS) while launching until the splash closes to skip scanning. Alt/Option+Rescan discards and rebuilds the plug-in database; it is a separate, approval-gated recovery action, not an ordinary refresh.
5. After a scan crash, Live can identify the offending VST on relaunch and offer rescan or exclusion; another crash can exclude it until reinstalled. Report the offending plug-in rather than repeatedly forcing scans. Legacy Intel Macs cannot natively load PowerPC-only VST/AU builds; architecture compatibility otherwise needs verification.
- Up to **64** modifiable parameters populate Live's plug-in panel automatically; more than 64 opens an empty configurable panel. Unfold the device to inspect it. The X-Y field's two choosers assign two exposed parameters for simultaneous control.
- **Show/Hide Plug-In Window** opens the manufacturer's interface; changes there and in Live's panel affect the same parameter. **Auto-Open Plug-In Windows**, **Multiple Plug-In Windows**, and **Auto-Hide Plug-In Windows** govern loading, concurrent windows, and selected-track visibility. Ctrl (Windows)/Cmd (macOS) while opening preserves previous windows even with Multiple disabled.
- **View → Show/Hide Plug-In Windows**, Ctrl+Alt+P (Windows)/Cmd+Option+P (macOS), toggles windows already opened manually or via Auto-Open; it does not initialize unopened panels. Window titles identify the owning track.
- To expose a control: enable **Configure**, then click or, where necessary, move the control in the plug-in window. Drag panel controls to reorder. Unpublished parameters cannot be exposed this way. Instances keep independent configurations saved with the Set; save a containing Rack for reuse.
- In Configure, select a panel control and press Delete only for an approved removal; this can affect automation, envelopes, MIDI/key mappings, and Macros; Live warns when these exist. Plug-ins without a native window expose only Live's panel, where Configure permits reordering but not removing controls.
- Without Configure, touching a native-window control creates temporary envelope/automation/X-Y chooser entries that disappear when another parameter is adjusted. Make an entry persistent by editing its envelope, selecting another parameter in an envelope chooser, or selecting the temporary control for X-Y. Recording parameter changes adds automated controls to the panel when recording stops; touching controls in MIDI/key/Macro mapping mode adds and selects them immediately.
- Exposed controls support mapping; continuous plug-in parameters support clip modulation/automation. Multiple plug-in inputs/outputs require appropriate track routing rather than assuming one stereo stream. Custom Info text and ordinary device moves/copies are available.
- For a supported plug-in's sidechain, select a Live routing point in its left-side choosers. **Gain** changes the detector input, not the source's audible mix level. **Mix = 100%** uses only the external trigger; **0%** effectively bypasses it. **Mute** bypasses the sidechain source input so you can check the plug-in output alone; the sidechain path itself is a trigger, not a separately mixed audible signal.
- VST preset/bank controls, where provided: the chooser below the title selects a preset in that instance's fixed-size bank; edits modify the selected preset, not a shared Live preset. **Rename Plug-In Preset** renames it. **Load Preset or Bank** imports; **Save Preset or Bank** exports the selected preset or full bank according to the format/file-type choice. Do not assume every VST3/native preset system offers the same controls.
- AU presets are browser-accessible; some factory entries appear only after inserting the AU and enabling Hot-Swap. Some are read-only. Their manufacturer/plug-in hierarchy is under the user's `Library/Audio/Presets`; inspect actual files rather than inferring an extension. AU mode/quality selectors may exist only in the native window. VST presets are not exposed in the browser in the same way as AU presets.

## Racks: parallel chains and zones
- Choose **MIDI Effect Rack** for MIDI-only processing; **Audio Effect Rack** for audio-only processing; **Instrument Rack** for MIDI effects → instrument → audio effects; **Drum Rack** for note-addressed instrument chains plus optional effect returns. Audio Effect Racks on MIDI tracks belong after the instrument.
- Devices within a chain run serially; Instrument/Effect Rack chains receive the same input in parallel and their outputs combine. Drum chains instead filter assigned input notes. A Rack acts as one device in its parent chain and can contain nested Racks.
- Create an empty Rack from the browser, or select device title bars and **Group**/**Group to Drum Rack**. Grouping again or grouping existing chains creates a nested Rack. Rack end brackets show ownership. **Ungroup** dismantles the selected Rack; preserve mappings before an approved structural change.
- Use the far-left view selectors for Macros, Chain List, Devices, and (Drum Rack only) Pads. Selecting the Rack title targets the entire Rack rather than one child. Rename/Edit Info Text describe the container. Folding hides its views into a narrow column.
- To find a deeply nested device, right-click the **Device View selector** and choose it from the hierarchy. Select a Chain List entry to inspect its devices; up/down navigates chains, and multiselection supports copying/regrouping. Drag into the list's empty area to add a chain.
- Chains can move between Racks/tracks; hovering over a destination track reveals its Device View. Each chain has Activator, Solo, and Hot-Swap; audio-producing chains also have volume/pan. Chains can be browser presets, renamed, colored, and given Info text.
- **Auto Select** selects chains processing signals; Drum Racks select on the assigned incoming note, while Instrument/Effect Racks reflect active zones. Use it to diagnose a silent split before changing devices.

| Zone/control | Use and boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Key | Instrument/MIDI Effect Racks only: restrict a chain to notes in its keyboard range, up to the full MIDI range (nearly 11 octaves). Key fades attenuate incoming note velocity. |
| Velocity | Instrument/MIDI Effect Racks only: accept Note On velocities **1–127**. Velocity fades also attenuate incoming velocity. |
| Chain Select | Instrument and Effect Racks: selector **0–127** chooses overlapping ranges. Default zones occupy one value, **0**. MIDI Effect Rack fades reduce incoming velocity; Instrument/Audio Effect Rack fades reduce chain output audio. |
| Combined filters | A note must pass every applicable Key, Velocity, and Chain Select zone. Audio Effect Racks have no Key/Velocity zones; Drum Racks have no zone editors. |

- Open **Key**, **Vel**, or **Chain** above the Chain List; **Hide** closes the editor. Drag the lower zone body to move it, its edges to resize it, and the narrow upper range edges to set fades.
- Instrument/MIDI Effect Rack chain selection filters notes but normally lets other MIDI events, including CCs, through. Enable **Chain Selector Filters MIDI Ctrl** on the Chain Select ruler's context menu only when those events must be filtered too.
- For a switchable bank, put each setup in a chain and give it a distinct one-value Chain Select zone, e.g. 0, 1, 2, 3. Map/control the selector within the occupied range; positions outside all zones admit no new input.
- For a morphing bank, widen neighboring zones to overlap and add complementary fades, retaining an exclusive position for each setup when needed. An eight-selector-step overlap is one possible transition width, not a requirement; widen it for finer travel. Check the summed level across the transition.
- Tail behavior differs: leaving a zone **with a fade** attenuates its output to zero; leaving a zone **without a fade** permits an already-playing tail to decay according to its devices. Choose fades deliberately rather than assuming preset changes always preserve tails.

## Drum Racks and chain extraction
- Show **Input/Output**, **Send**, **Return**, and **Auto Select** as needed. **Receive** selects the incoming note (with note number/GM hints); **Play** sets the outgoing instrument note. **Preview** triggers a test note without a keyboard.
- Assign related chains to one of **16 Choke groups** when a new hit should silence the others. **Receive = All Notes** passes incoming pitch through and disables Play and Choke. Check this setting before diagnosing missing choking or unexpected pitch.
- Add up to **six audio-effect return chains** in the separate return area to expose drum-chain Send controls. Sends are post-fader. A return's **Audio To** routes to the Rack's main output or directly to a Set return track.
- The **128** pads represent notes, not individual chains. The overview moves the visible bank by **16** pads with drag/up/down; Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS) shifts by a single row. A pad represents every nested chain able to receive its note.
- Drop a sample on an empty pad to create a sample-playing chain (normally Simpler, subject to defaults); drop an audio effect on it to place that effect after the instrument. Dropping a replacement sample replaces Simpler/sample while leaving surrounding MIDI/audio effects intact.
- Dropping multiple selected samples distributes new sample chains chromatically upward from the target pad and can replace existing samples there, while keeping effects. Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS)-drop instead layers the samples in a nested Instrument Rack on one pad. Inspect the target range before replacement.
- Dragging a pad to another pad swaps note assignments; existing MIDI clips are **not** rewritten to preserve their former sounds. Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS)-drag layers both pads' chains into a nested Instrument Rack. Editing Receive directly also updates the pads; several chains on the same Receive note layer together.
- In Hot-Swap mode, **D** toggles the target between the Drum Rack and the last selected pad. Empty pads show their note/GM hint; single-chain pads expose name, mute, solo, Preview, and Hot-Swap. A **Multi** pad controls all its chains and reflects mixed mute/solo states, but disables pad renaming and Hot-Swap; inspect its chains individually. Removing a Multi pad removes all its represented chains, not just the visible one.
- A natively supported pad controller selected under **Link, Tempo & MIDI → Control Surface** can follow the visible Drum Rack pad bank when the track receives MIDI. Do not assume this behavior for an arbitrary mapped controller.
- Unfold a multichain Instrument/Drum Rack using the Session track-header control to mix its chains alongside tracks. Nested chains can unfold too. These are chain strips, not tracks with clip slots; routing/mix changes mirror the Chain List. Click a chain title to show its devices.
- Session-mixer multiselection links adjustments of the same mixer parameter across selected chains. That linked-adjustment behavior does **not** apply when making the adjustment in the Rack's Chain List.
- To separate a drum sound **with its MIDI notes**, drag its chain's title from the unfolded **Session mixer** to the mixer's new-track drop area. To move **devices only**, drag from the **Chain List**. Other chains can move into existing tracks/Racks; extracting a Drum Rack return into the mixer creates a Set return track. Verify both routing and note ownership after extraction.

## Macros and variations
1. A Rack supports **16 Macros**, with **eight** initially shown. Use the increase/decrease view controls to show the needed count; visibility is saved with the Set. A Macro can control many parameters across that Rack's devices.
2. Enable **Map**, click a highlighted mappable parameter, then the desired Macro's **Map** button. In the Mapping Browser, set Min/Max. Min greater than Max, or **Invert Range**, reverses direction. Repeat for other parameters and exit Map mode.
3. Mapped device controls appear disabled because the Macro owns their absolute value; clip envelopes can still modulate them. Diagnose a disabled control by inspecting Macro mappings before assuming the device is broken. Re-enter Map to edit/remove assignments with appropriate scope.
4. One assignment supplies its name/units to the Macro. Multiple assignments restore a generic Macro name and usually a **0–127** scale; common units remain only when all targets share both unit type and range. Set useful names/colors/Info text and optionally MIDI-map the Macro for performance.
- **Rand** changes mapped Macro values. Use **Exclude Macro from Randomization** for controls that should stay fixed; Volume-targeting Macros in Instrument Rack presets are excluded by default. Do not infer that every loudness-affecting mapping is excluded.
- Open **Macro Variations**, then **New** to capture current Macro values. **Launch Macro Variation** recalls a snapshot; **Overwrite Macro Variation** replaces it. Variations can be named, duplicated, or removed with explicit scope; they are Macro states, not arbitrary whole-device snapshots.
- **Exclude Macro From Variations** keeps a Macro unchanged when another variation launches. This is separate from randomization exclusion; disabling it restores variation recall for that Macro. Check exclusion states when a recall appears incomplete.

## Automation: record, override, and inspect
- Automation specifies absolute values over time; modulation influences those values. Automated controls normally have red/theme-dependent LEDs; blue indicates modulation. A dashed envelope line means the parameter has no automation yet and displays its current value.

| Goal | UI procedure | Result/check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Record into Session clips | Enable **Automation Arm**, arm relevant tracks, then **Session Record**; move controls. **Record, Warp & Launch → Record Session automation in → All Tracks** permits recording into all playing clips without arming each track. | Clip-based envelopes; All Tracks can overdub automation in existing MIDI clips without also arming for new notes. Session Record stops recording while playback continues; Stop ends both. |
| Record Arrangement automation | Enable **Automation Arm**, then **Arrangement Record** and move controls. Track arming is not required for automation: it records across all tracks. | Track-based envelopes, including tempo. Arrangement Record stops recording while playback continues; Stop ends both. |
| Capture Session performance to Arrangement | Enable Automation Arm and use Arrangement recording while performing in Session. | Existing Session clip envelopes and new control movements become Arrangement track automation. Copying an automated Session clip into Arrangement also transfers its automation to the track. |
| Try another value without replacing data | Stop recording, then adjust the automated parameter. | Its LED goes gray and **Re-Enable Automation** lights; the stored envelope remains. Restore one parameter with its context menu, all overridden automation with the Control Bar button, or Session clip automation by relaunching its clip. |

- While recording, further movement of the same parameter overwrites existing automation. In Session overdubs, mouse editing is **touch**: release returns to recorded values; a MIDI knob/fader is **latch**: the last value holds to clip/loop end. A Session clip's **first** automation recording uses latch even with the mouse.
- Session inspection: open Clip View's Envelope Editor, select **Automation**, then Device and Control. **Show Adjusted Envelopes Only** combines the choosers into Automated Control; **Show All Envelopes** restores them. Stacked Clip/Device views let a clicked control select its envelope.
- Arrangement inspection: enable **Automation Mode** above headers or press A; choose Device and Automation Control in the main lane. **None** hides the envelope and empties its control chooser. Click a device/mixer parameter, or use **Show Automation**/**Show Automation in New Lane** to focus it.
- **Show Automated Parameters Only** combines Arrangement choosers; **Show All Parameters** restores them. In either filtered view, clicking an unautomated control will not reveal a new envelope. Arrangement's Show Automation commands are disabled/unavailable in that case; an unautomated envelope already displayed can remain visible.
- **Add Automation Lane** moves the main-lane envelope into an additional lane and sets the main Device chooser to None; choose another parameter to inspect both. The add button returns after selecting a real parameter. **Add Lane for Each Automated Envelope** exposes all existing envelopes; Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS)-clicking Add does the same.
- Additional lanes can be reordered by dragging, focused with up/down, and hidden/shown with the main-lane toggle or left/right from the main lane. Left from an additional lane returns to the main lane. If the main lane is None, hiding extra lanes promotes the topmost one into it.
- Selecting a parameter already shown in another lane focuses that lane. Ctrl (Windows)/Option (macOS)-click Add can create up to **two duplicate lanes** for the main envelope; these are views of the same data, not independent envelopes.
- **Remove Automation Lane** removes the selected extra lane views. Ctrl/Cmd-click or Shift-click headers selects several; Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS)-click Remove affects that lane and all below. Do not confuse removing views with **Clear Envelope** commands that erase automation data.

## Automation: construct and edit
1. Select the correct parameter and verify its units/range. With Draw Mode off, click its dashed/solid line to insert a breakpoint, drag to choose its value, or double-click elsewhere to add one there. **Add Value** in the line's context menu supports exact entry. The first point turns a dashed line solid.
2. Grid snapping is normal; hold Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS) to bypass it for point insertion/movement. Shift while dragging vertically gives finer value control; Shift while dragging horizontally restricts movement to time. Hover points to inspect values.
3. For stepped gestures, enable **Draw Mode** with B/the Control Bar toggle; holding B temporarily enables it when off. Each step follows visible grid width. Disable **Snap to Grid** with Ctrl+4/Cmd+4, or hold Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS) while drawing, for unsnapped movement.
4. For a pattern, create a time selection and choose a shape from the editor/lane context menu. It fills the selection in time and the parameter range vertically; without a time selection it fills one current grid unit. Available shapes include sine, triangle, sawtooth, inverse sawtooth, square, two ramp sets, and ADSR.
5. Waveforms insert their own shape regardless of adjoining values; ramps adapt to values at the selection boundaries. Discrete-state controls create stepped rather than diagonal envelopes and permit only the square preset shape.
- Drag a breakpoint to move it; click it to remove it and reconnect neighbors. Drag the background to select several, then drag a selected point to move them together. **Edit Value** sets a point/segment numerically; for multiple selected points the edited point takes the entered value and others shift relatively.
- To move a segment, drag near but not directly on its line. Alternatively Shift-click the line, hold the mouse, release Shift, then drag. Shift-drag vertically is fine adjustment; horizontally it constrains time movement. Selection-edge breakpoints are inserted when a selected segment moves, including when moved outside its original time selection.
- Dragging onto existing points replaces those points only on release; inspect the preview before committing. For a diagonal segment, Alt (Windows)/Option (macOS)-drag adds curvature; Alt/Option-double-click straightens it. Horizontal/vertical segments do not accept that curve operation. macOS **Cmd** for grid bypass and **Option** for curvature are different modifiers.
- For a selected time span, use top/bottom center handles to scale values (Shift for fine control), left/right center handles to stretch time, and corners to skew. Alt (Windows)/Cmd (macOS) during horizontal movement bypasses snapping. Alt (Windows)/Option (macOS) mirrors the opposite handle's movement. Expansion across points outside the selection removes them; preserve neighboring data first.
- Select a dense passage and choose **Simplify Envelope** to reduce redundant points using lines/curves. Verify the resulting motion, not just the lower point count.
- For approved removal, **Delete Automation** on a parameter (Ctrl+Backspace on Windows/Cmd+Delete on macOS) clears its automation, removes its LED, and returns the value to its default. **Clear Envelope** targets the selected envelope; **Clear All Envelopes of…** targets a device from a lane header. Broad **Clear All Envelopes** can remove Set automation: do not use it as a local repair.
- For tempo automation, use Arrangement **Main → Mixer → Song Tempo**, or record Control Bar tempo changes with Arrangement Record/Automation Arm. **Tempo Minimum/Maximum Range** sets the BPM display range and also the range of a MIDI controller mapped to tempo. Session clip automation is not the direct tempo-automation destination.
- Moving Arrangement clips normally moves their automation. Enable **Lock Envelopes** in Options/the Arrangement toggle when automation must stay fixed to the timeline; verify this state before edits or video pre-roll restoration.
- A time selection **inside automation lanes** scopes Cut/Copy/Duplicate/Delete to envelope segments, not the clip; selections can span several lanes. Copy/paste can cross parameters with different units/ranges, so inspect the destination values instead of assuming a meaningful translation.

## Clip envelopes: relative control and independent loops
- Open Clip View's **Envelopes** tab, then Device and Control. Audio clips offer **Clip**, track effects, and Mixer; MIDI clips offer **MIDI Ctrl**, track devices, and Mixer. Altered-envelope LEDs and the adjusted-only chooser filter help locate existing work.
- Session clips offer **Automation** and **Modulation** toggles. Arrangement clips have clip modulation here; their absolute automation is in track lanes. Red/theme-colored automation and blue modulation can coexist for one control; knob position shows the absolute value and a blue ring segment shows modulation.
- Use the same breakpoint/draw/shape methods as automation. **Clear Envelope** resets only the chosen clip envelope; inspect that selection before clearing. **MIDI Envelope Auto-Reset** in Options/the Sample Editor context menu can reset certain MIDI control messages at new-clip start; do not assume every CC is reset.
- Audio clip modulation is computed during playback without altering the source sample. Rendering/resampling can print it; Arrangement Consolidate can create a new sample. To reuse an envelope design, select the clip and drop another sample onto **Clip View**: sample changes, clip settings/envelopes remain.

| Desired change | Set up and interpret |
| --- | --- |
| Per-note audio pitch | Enable **Warp**; choose **Clip → Transposition**. Draw steps for note regions, then refine ramps/points; Shift gives finer tuning. Modulation adds to Transpose, with the final result limited to **−48…+48 semitones**. |
| Faster pitch-envelope response | Use smaller **Grain Size** in Tones/Texture or finer **Granulation Resolution** in Beats; warp settings limit how closely playback follows the curve. Ctrl+Alt (Windows)/Cmd+Option (macOS)-drag scrolls the envelope display. |
| Gate/attenuate before effects | **Clip → Gain** scales Clip Gain by a relative percentage, down to silence but never above its absolute gain setting. |
| Gate/attenuate after effects | **Mixer → Track Volume** modulates the mixer stage. The small dot beneath the volume fader indicates the resulting level; the fader remains the absolute baseline. |
| Animate effect-send amount | Modulate a Mixer Send as a relative percentage; it can reduce to −∞ dB but cannot exceed the Send knob setting. The blue ring shows the modulated result. |
| Pan movement | At centered pan, modulation can reach hard left/right. Moving the base pan toward either extreme reduces modulation depth; at hard left it has no effect. |
| Device movement | Modulation operates relative to each device control's current/automated setting. It is not a device preset and does not establish an independent absolute value. |
| Reorder beat-loop playback | Use **Beats** warp mode, then **Clip → Sample Offset**. Each vertical increment is a **sixteenth note**, spanning **−8 to +8 sixteenths**; positive reads ahead, negative reads behind. Draw nonzero steps. For precise cut/splice editing, use Arrangement edits instead. |
| Repetition/slur from offset | A descending staircase can repeat the initial step; a smooth descending ramp can slow the apparent traversal. A **1/32 Granulation Resolution** is an available fine-grained experiment, not a universal setting. |
| Send MIDI control data | On a MIDI clip select **MIDI Ctrl**, then a controller; draw/edit its envelope, including recorded/imported data. Most controller numbers through **119** are available. The destination's implementation determines what a named control actually does. |

- When automation fades a baseline down while modulation rises, modulation only uses the range still allowed by that baseline; it cannot defeat the absolute fade. Diagnose unexpectedly small modulation by inspecting both layers.
- **Linked** envelopes share the clip's timing; turn off Linked to expose independent envelope region/loop controls, indicated by the colored loop brace. While linked, moving Warp Markers stretches/shrinks the linked envelope, and markers can be edited from the Envelope Editor. Unlink only when envelope timing must be independent.
- To fade a one-bar audio loop over **eight bars**: choose Clip Gain or Mixer Track Volume, unlink the envelope, leave the sample loop enabled, disable the envelope's own Loop, set its region length to eight bars, and draw from the starting level to silence at the region end. Zoom out with the envelope ruler to see both endpoints. The envelope is one-shot while audio keeps looping.
- To repeat a longer movement over a shorter sample, enable the unlinked envelope's own Loop instead. Extend braces beyond the visible area or enter region/loop values numerically. Each envelope can have its own length, including nonmatching values such as `3.2.1`; start markers define launch reference points for both sample and envelope.
- For rhythmic gating of a long sample, use a short independently looping Gain/Volume envelope; the audio need not share its loop length. For an LFO-like control shape, loop an envelope locally and use shapes/stretch/skew. It follows project tempo, but off-grid loop endpoints can avoid meter-aligned repetition.
- Envelope start/end markers, braces, and drawing follow the zoom-adaptive grid. Disable the grid when deliberate off-grid timing is needed. Verify at launch and across several sample/envelope cycles, especially with mismatched lengths.

## Video scoring and delivery
1. Import an Apple QuickTime `.mov` by dragging it from the browser into an **Arrangement audio track**. Video in Session is treated as audio only. Confirm the filmstrip-style title markings and a visible Video Window before assuming picture is present.
2. Show/hide the floating **Video Window** from View; drag to position and resize from its lower-right corner. Its location/size is application state, not Set-specific. Double-click for full screen (including another monitor); Alt (Windows)/Option (macOS)-double-click restores the video's original size.
3. Add music on a separate Arrangement track. Open the video in Clip View, enable **Warp**, and set **Leader/Follower = Leader** to make music follow video timing rather than warp the picture to music. Video defaults to leader and audio to follower in this scoring setup, but verify the actual states.
4. Add/move video Warp Markers at hit points; the Video Window updates to the corresponding frame during movement, and embedded QuickTime markers can act as cues. Loop a section to check synchronization. If several playing warped clips are leaders, only the **bottom-most currently playing** one actually leads; another video can therefore be warped unintentionally.
5. With an approved render range, use **File → Export Audio/Video** for a mixed audio file and, where supported by the local export setup, video. Verify export options locally; file extension alone is not proof that the installed video pipeline can decode or encode it.
- Trim a video by its Arrangement edges. **Consolidate, Reverse, and Crop replace it internally with audio-only material**; the original movie file is unchanged, but the Set loses that clip's picture. Avoid these commands when video must remain attached.
- QuickTime audio/video components may cover different portions of the file: a video gap displays black; an audio gap produces silence. Distinguish such intentional gaps from failed import or routing.
- For a temporary scoring start without pre-roll: place the movie at **1.1.1**, then move its **Clip Start Marker** past the pre-roll so action starts at **1.1.1 / 00:00:00:00**. This is a trim, not removal from the movie.
- To restore delivery pre-roll, first preserve a recoverable Set and check Lock Envelopes. With approval, shift **all composition material together** far enough right; deselect everything except the video and extend its left edge back to expose the original pre-roll. Check music alignment after the move.
- Export duration normally follows the Arrangement selection. Selecting the restored, otherwise full-duration video can make the audio render match the original movie duration including pre-roll. Verify both endpoints explicitly, especially if the video was otherwise trimmed.

## Verification and recovery
- **No sound:** inspect input/arm/monitoring, MIDI-versus-audio device order, chain Activators/Solo, Key/Velocity/Chain ranges or Receive notes, then inter-device meters. Correct the first failed stage rather than replacing the whole chain.
- **Control will not follow its envelope:** check recording/override state, A/B state, active Session versus Arrangement ownership, Macro mappings, and whether you edited automation or modulation. Re-enable only the intended automation.
- **Wrong sound after pad work:** inspect Receive/Play assignments and any replacement range; pad swaps do not rewrite clips. For extraction, compare MIDI notes as well as devices and returns.
- **Preset/default loads differently:** distinguish active A/B state, plain preset versus default, User Library versus Project precedence, plug-in configuration versus stored values, and missing referenced samples.
- **Timing changed:** compare Delay Compensation/Reduced Latency When Monitoring, tempo leadership, Lock Envelopes, linked/unlinked loop lengths, and the current grid. These solve different timing problems and are not interchangeable.
- Verify changes with the actual UI state and relevant playback/meters or render inspection when authorized. Record what was checked, what was merely configured, and what remains untested; do not claim listening or successful execution from these procedures alone.
